Teaching Philosophy

Effective education fosters learning environments where all students feel valued, represented, and respected. Teaching approaches that center anti-oppressive, inclusive, and culturally sustaining practices encourage critical thinking and support equitable learning outcomes.

Reflective and collaborative practices help ensure that every learner experiences a sense of belonging and agency. These approaches align with curriculum expectations while emphasizing social justice, diversity, and inclusion in every aspect of teaching.

Prioritize Equity

Ensure every student feels valued and included. Challenge bias and center anti-oppressive practices in your teaching.

Foster Collaboration

Encourage peer dialogue, cooperative learning, and empathy in the classroom to build a supportive community.

Reflect Regularly

Continuously reflect on your teaching practices, lesson planning, and classroom interactions to improve inclusion and learning outcomes.

Sample Lessons

Equity-Focused Lesson Plans

These sample lessons demonstrate how anti-oppressive education can be integrated into Ontario curriculum subjects.

Language Arts

Students explore Indigenous language and perspectives, promoting respect and valuing knowledge beyond Western ways.

Social Studies

This lesson helps students recognize and reflect on privilege in media and society, encouraging them to promote equity.

Arts Integration

Students explore socio-political issues through sculpture, using art to express perspectives on equity, justice, and social change.

Science

Students explore fair access to clean water and develop actions to conserve resources, highlighting equity and community responsibility.

Math

Students will use math and data to explore fair trade, understand fairness in wages and profits, and see how their choices impact people globally.

Health

Students explore cultural dishes and categorize their ingredients into food groups, promoting healthy eating and respect for diversity.
